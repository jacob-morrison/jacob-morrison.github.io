Hi! I work on making language models more capable and more open, and on understanding their environmental and societal impacts. I'm a Ph.D. student at the University of Washington, where I'm advised by Noah Smith and supported by an NSF Computer Science Graduate Fellowship. I'm also a student researcher on the Open Ecosystem team at Ai2, where I help build the Olmo family of fully open language models, and an AI Fellow at Madrona Venture Group.

Before my Ph.D., I worked on code generation at Google [x] and platform health at Twitter, and in a previous life I was a software engineer at Tableau and Google. See my CV for more details.

What I work on

Open language models

I'm a core contributor to Ai2's Olmo project, which releases strong language models together with the training data, code, and infrastructure behind them. Much of my work is on data-centric methods for improving model capabilities, including the Tülu post-training recipes and the RewardBench evaluations for reward models.

Environmental & societal impacts of AI

I study what it actually costs to build and run language models, in energy, water, and carbon, from hardware manufacturing through deployment. More recently that includes the added footprint of reasoning models.

AI law & policy

I spend part of my time helping policymakers understand and address the impacts of AI. I've served as a public policy lead at Ai2, testified before the Washington State House Consumer Protection & Business Committee, served on the City of Seattle's Generative AI Policy Advisory Group, helped organize the Future of AI Forum with U.S. Senator Maria Cantwell's office, and lobbied state lawmakers as a UW Alumni Association district representative. I've also written about legal questions raised by generative AI, such as liability, legal risk, and the First Amendment. You can find that work on my publications page.
